Why we are using an AML dataset to teach this

It has the shape real ML problems have

Alert triage is supervised binary classification on labelled historical dispositions, and it carries every awkward property you actually have to handle in production.

The classes are imbalanced. The labels are noisy, because some genuine cases were closed incorrectly. Features have to be computed as of the alert date or they leak the future.

If you can do this one properly, the rest of your ML work is easier, not harder.

And it has a real blind spot to find

The legacy rules in this dataset are per-account amount thresholds. Two typologies are built so those rules cannot see them.

A per-account threshold cannot detect a pattern defined across accounts, like a shared collector wallet. It cannot detect activity that nets to zero, like same-day offsetting trades.

That gives the model something genuine to learn, rather than a reconstruction of the rules it is meant to improve on.

The data is synthetic and generated for this lab. Every number you see today comes from your own build, not from a slide.

Two things worth arguing about before we start

Accuracy is the wrong metric here

On an imbalanced dataset, a model that predicts "false positive" for every single alert scores well on accuracy while being worth nothing. You will see this happen with your own numbers.

We will use precision-recall instead, and we will look at one measure above all others: true positives found at fixed review capacity.

Analyst capacity is fixed. You review 500 alerts a week either way. The question is which 500.

Explainability is the deliverable, not a feature

A score of 0.87 cannot go in a SAR narrative. This can:

Elevated because inbound volume is 14x declared expected volume, 9 transactions in the 8k-10k band in 30 days, and the primary outbound counterparty is shared with 6 other platform accounts.

The model does not replace the investigator's judgment. It writes the first paragraph of their narrative and points them at the evidence.
